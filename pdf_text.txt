Project Abstract 
 
Motorcycle accidents remain an important public health concern around the world,  
and Pakistan reports more than 10,000 deaths annually due to road accidents, which 
often involve two-wheeled vehicles. The HelmX project aims to address this pressing 
challenge by developing an AI -backed system for motorcycles that aims to improve 
rider safety, navigation, and the riding experience with real -time contextual 
observational monitoring and intelligent feedback. The system will be comprised of 
seven purposefully constructed main features: (1) Crash Detection utilizing sensors 
embedded within the device such as accelerometers and gyroscopes to determine 
impacts, automatically notifying the emergency services via GPS; (2) Drowsiness 
Detection using com puter vision algorithms on front -facing camera images and 
videos of the rider identifying both eye closure and head pose, which will generate 
both audio and visual alerts; (3) Smart Navigation which optimizes real -time 
recommendations and route suggestions  by avoiding traffic and incorporating 
weather recommendations ; (4) Capture images or video s with the Dual Cameras 
(front and rear) of the motorcycle that can constantly record veterinary observable 
evidence, or at the request of the rider via voice comman d to provide images of the 
environment; (5) Voice Control and Entertainment Systems capable of performing 
online and offline commands for media playback, calls, and system controls, 
adaptable to ambient sound conditions; (6) Safety and Environment Monitori ng 
System measuring air quality, temperature, and allergens; they will ultimately 
generate ride recommendations ; (7) Complete Web and android application  for ride 
analytics. 
Optional features, such as gesture -based controls and posture correction, fuel 
estimation, and AI safety tutorials for maintenance, are expected to be prioritized 
based on the projected timelines for development. Elements that are not addressed in 
the context of the project include large -scale manufacturing of hardware, medical 
validation of the health metrics, and advanced hardware like dynamic visor tint..  
HelmX employs IoT sensors, AI algorithms, and user-centric interfaces, with the aim 
of preventing accidents through alerts and information -driven conclusions to reduce 
risk by a considerable perc entage. Rather than purely an academic initiative, this 
project will have implications for sustainable transport systems of urban mobility in 
developing nations.  
Researchers will measure the results of the project based on the prototype's 
functionality, accuracy of user testing (> 85% for detection), and supervisor/client 
agreement. The research aims to enable riders the technology to prioritize safety 
while not detracting from the positively perceived thrill of riding.

1. Introduction 
Motorcycle riders are among the most vulnerable users on the road, accounting for a 
disproportionately high number of global traffic fatalities. The primary challenges 
include limited visibility, rider fatigue, and t he cognitive overload of navigating 
while operating a vehicle. Existing solutions, such as standard helmets and basic 
Bluetooth devices, offer fragmented features but lack an integrated, intelligent 
system capable of proactive accident prevention and post-incident response. 
Helm X is a comprehensive solution for addressing safety challenges of motorcycle 
riders. This project will design and develop a smart helmet that employs the use of 
Artificial Intelligence and the internet of things to establish a smart er, intuitive riding 
ecosystem. The specific goals of the project are to: 1) Create a robust hardware 
prototype with embedded cameras, inertial measurement units (IMU) , a battery and 
other related things ; 2) Implement the core AI algorithms to detect drowsiness, 
analyze accidents, and utilize computer vision 3) Establish secure cloud 
infrastructure for storing data and alerting in real-time; and 4) Create web and mobile 
interfaces for visualizing data and enabling controls of the systems. Success will be 
measured by the accurate functioning of the core AI models, the seamless integration 
of all hardware and software components, and positive feedback from user 
acceptance testing. 
     
2. Success Criterion 
The following verifiable criteria will outline the success of the Helm X initiative:  
 
1. Functional Prototype:  A working prototype of the helmet will be  delivered 
with the successful implementation of at least the s even required core features 
(Crash Detection,  Drowsiness Detection, Navigation, Dual Cameras, Voice 
Control and Entertainment System, Safety System and Environment Monitoring 
System).  
2. AI Accuracy: The accuracy and recall rate of the drowsiness detection system 
and crash detection system must be >90%  at least in controlled testing 
environments and the crash detection algorithm must achieve a false positive 
rate of <10%.  
3. System Integration: The Android application and web portal will successfully 
communicate with the prototype helmet displaying real -time data analytics, 
historical analytics, and system configuration.  
4. User Acceptance : The system must receive a positive score (e.g., >70 on the 
System Usability Scale) from a pilot group of at least 20 test riders.  
5. Documentation: Final submission must in clude complete technical 
documentation and a research paper on the system's architecture and treatment 
of AI.

3. Related work 
 
Research into motorcycle safety systems has thus far identified the promise of using                       
AI, but does demonstrate opportunities for integrated, cost-effective solutions, thanks               
to further collaboration within the developing context.  
 
• In 2025, Kim et al. create d an AI -based smart motorcycle helmet that provided 
drowsiness detection through EEG signals and facial recognition detection with a 
92% accuracy, but was limited to helmet use without a navigation option [1].  
• A 2023 Sentiance implementation demonstrated crash detection features using a 
mobile device's sensors to provide alerts about a crash in real time. Their 
approach appealed to privacy preserving ML while lacking good modeling and 
safety system integrations in their approach [2].  
• A 2025 study conducte d by Chigee, detailing an AIO -6 system to enhance 
CarPlay AI, evaluated improved routing to destination through rider behavior, 
but their AI based system did not provide rider ergonomics like posture 
monitoring systems [3].  
• The 2024 Rexing MTC1 study prov ides dual camera angle recording with a 
1080p definition and allows for evidence collection, but it relies on perceptive 
video evidence without AI or event tagging implementations [4].  
• The 2024 study on voice and different entertainment systems highlighted the 
advantages with Bluetooth controls and heads -up displays, however, adaptive 
audio systems based on the traffic flow and noise level currently remain 
unexplored [5]. 
• The IoT-based crash alert system explored by Sharma et al. (2023) applies ML 
systems to high-definition data compilations, while adding systems based on our 
work with sensor fusion in designing a motorcycle safety system [6].  
• The concept of AR for navigation in helmets has been pioneered by companies 
like BMW, iC and Skully, but their solutions are prohibitively expensive and not 
tailored for the broader market [7]. 
 
 
Overall, there are features that meet the academic standards, however, we have yet to 
create a comprehensive system that integrates core safety features with the added 
details, for riders from low -resource communities.  HelmX will go  a step further, 
combining these disparate features into a single, integrated, and cost -effective 
solution. Unlike comm ercially available products, Helm X will  emphasize open, 
modular design and utilizes the latest deep learning models to enhance accuracy in 
real-world settings. Additionally, the development of a full -stack analytical platform 
provides a value proposition n ot seen with available products or prototypes .

4. Project Rationale 
HelmX evolved out of an urgency to apply technology to save lives in motorcycle 
accidents. Motorcycles are 28 times more likely to result in fatalities than cars 
because motorcyclists are oft en immobilized or unconscious before they can be 
evaluated and treated. The initiative is relevant because it will address this gap by 
providing instant automated alerts. The project is also intended to reduce the 
cognitive load on riders and provide situa tional awareness while navigating and 
performing other tasks in order to make riding less distracting while riding safer.  
Ultimately, we are excited to execute a project that allows us to apply our expertise 
and education related to AI , cloud and software engineering to a contemporary issue 
that has social impact and social significance. We hope to learn about embedded 
systems integration, the challenges related to deploying AI in resource -constrained 
settings, and full stack product development processes specifically for ridesharing.

5. Proposed Methodology and Architecture 
The project will follow an Agile development methodology with 2–4 week 
sprints. The system architecture is divided into four layers: 
 
• Hardware Layer:  In first phase complete hardware required will be 
collected. The helmet unit equipped with camera s, IMU, G PS module, 
battery, and relevant things will be collected. 
• Edge AI Layer (Optional): On-device processing for real -time tasks 
(drowsiness detection, crash detection) using TensorFlow Lite models to 
minimize latency and cloud dependency. 
• Cloud Layer: A secure AWS IoT Core an d Node.js backend to handle data 
ingestion, storage (MongoDB), and send SMS/email alerts via Twilio API in 
case of a crash. 
• Application Layer: A React.js web dashboard and a Kotlin -based Android 
app for users to view rides, analytics, and configure settings.

  5.1 Algorithms:  
  A step-by-step process for a core feature, like crash detection, is:  
(i) IMU data is continuously read  
(ii) Data is fed into a pre -trained classifier on the edge 
device 
(iii) If a crash is predicted, the device triggers an alert 
(iv) GPS coordinates and crash data are sent to the cloud 
(v) The cloud service notifies emergency contacts and logs 
the event. 
 
 
 
 
        Similarly for drowsiness detection: 
(i) The process begins by receiving a constant video feed 
from the front-facing camera. 
(ii) The video feed is processed by a computer vision 
model, likely using libraries such a s OpenCV and a 
Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). 
(iii) The model extracts specific features from the video, 
such as eye closure and head pose. 
(iv) The extracted features are analyzed to determine if the 
rider is drowsy. 
(v) If drowsiness is detected, the system generates both 
audio and visual alerts to notify the rider.
